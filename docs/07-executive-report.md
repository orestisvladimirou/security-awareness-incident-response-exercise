# 07 — Executive briefing: fictional vendor-linked identity incident

**SIMULATED, NOT A REAL INCIDENT OR PERFORMED RESPONSE.** All company, date, system and user references are invented. Scenario date 18 November 2026, after dossier creation.

## Situation

A customer-support partner identity generates an unfamiliar sign-in alert. A staff member reports an unexpected vendor verification email and the support application records 41 ticket reads in nine minutes. The pattern warrants expedited investigation, but attribution, credential compromise, full session scope and data export remain **unproven**.

## Proposed response posture

- **Priority:** SEV-2 provisional, to be reassessed as facts become available.
- **Business trade-off:** temporary vendor access suspension may interrupt support; protect essential coverage through an approved fallback.
- **Key evidence missing:** reliable identity session export, vendor-side ticket export audit, personal-data scope, independently verified token revocation.
- **Privacy/legal:** refer to specialists without assuming breach notification is or is not required.
- **Recovery:** defer until authenticated access state, privilege scope and service-owner acceptance are proven.

## Educational deliverables

11 scenario events, eight proposed decisions, ten response actions, ten evidence references, six tabletop injects, four learning modules, eight assessment questions, six future metrics and an eight-sheet workbook. **None represent actual team activity.**

## Proposed leadership decisions

1. Approve incident-response and vendor contact ownership before running any drill.
2. Fund vendor session-audit retention and reviewed least-privilege role design.
3. Authorize a safe tabletop with a measured observation rubric, independent review and no production changes.
4. Pilot short role-based awareness modules and measure behaviors, not just attendance.
5. Require evidence and formal sign-off before closing actions and restoring compromised access in a genuine incident.

## Conclusion

The current synthetic record supports a strong *practice scenario* but no validated assertion of account compromise, actual disclosure, completed response, trained workforce, or improved resilience. The correct case-study outcome is an **open decision and evidence backlog**, not a fictional success story presented as fact.
