# 08 — Standards, sources, and limitations

## Official reference publications

- **NIST SP 800-61 Revision 3**, *Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management* (April 2025): https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final
- **NIST incident-response project overview** (CSF 2.0 integration; Detect / Respond / Recover with broader preparation): https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/incident-response
- **NIST SP 800-50 Revision 1**, *Building a Cybersecurity and Privacy Learning Program* (September 2024): https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/50/r1/final
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0**, risk-management reference: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework

Source themes are **independent paraphrases**, not reproduction of standards. We follow current Rev 3 guidance, not the superseded Rev 2 lifecycle as though it were the current standard. SP 800-50 is customizable learning-program guidance, **not certification**.

## Explicit assurance boundaries

- Entirely synthetic organizations, telemetry, messages, decisions and evidence.
- Dates 18–20 November 2026 are hypothetical future exercise dates relative to 5 November 2026 creation; no one actually completed these events.
- No real attack, phishing email, credential submission, employer deployment, log export, forensic chain of custody, employee quiz result, business authorization, vendor contact or regulatory report exists.
- Suspected access and 41 ticket reads **do not establish exfiltration**.
- No training performance has been measured. Every learning metric baseline is `NOT MEASURED`.
- All ten response actions are `OPEN - SIMULATED`. Actual authorization/closure cannot be inferred from a filled workbook or passing test.
- Data protection/regulatory legal decisions are external gated assessments, not performed here.

## Appropriate CV wording

**Independent simulated security awareness and incident-response case study:** developed a vendor-account-compromise scenario, evidence-based triage and containment/recovery playbook, six-inject tabletop guide, role-based training materials, and Python-validated incident/action registers. Do not describe it as handling a real incident or training real employees.
